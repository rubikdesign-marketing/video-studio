# Playbook: promo

A film that sells one offer or one launch. The viewer should want it and know what to do next.

| | |
|---|---|
| Length | 45–75 s. Cap agreed at the idea stage |
| Energy preset | hype for sales, confident for launches |
| Stage | Free. Night and colour-field stages suit sales |
| References to study first | `library/references/dokan/`, the launch films in `claude/`, `google/`, `elevenlabs/` |

## Shape

| Part | Share | Job | Energy |
|---|---|---|---|
| Hook | first 3–5 s | The offer, or a tension the viewer recognises. Spoken and on screen | 4–5 |
| Turn | 5–10% | Why now, or what changes | 2–3 |
| Proof | 50–60% | Three moments of the product doing the thing. Each: ask, result, hold | 3–4, rising |
| Peak | 5–8% | The signature moment | 5 |
| Offer | 10–15% | Price or promise, once, in fixed wording | 4 |
| Close | 4–6 s | Call to action, deadline if any, logo | 3, then rest |

## Rules

- Offer first when there is an offer. Never bury it.
- Full sentences spoken to a buyer. No list of features. Outcomes, not feature names.
- One price in the voice. Offer wording is fixed at the script approval and never varies.
- Three proofs, each a different shot size and a different transition.
- Promotional energy is allowed and wanted: confetti on the announcement, urgency, a real deadline.
- The logo opens and closes the film. It is not on the frames in between.

## The hook lab

For every promo, the first-animation step includes three different openings for the first three seconds, at preview quality. The maker picks one. The others are kept for testing as ad variants.

## First animation

The animatic of the whole film with the real voice, plus one proof scene and the hook at full polish.

## Signature moments that have worked

Recorded in `brands/<brand>/ledger.json` after each video, so the next promo does not repeat them.

## Cut-downs

Mark at the storyboard stage which beats make a 30 s, a 15 s and a 6 s version. They are produced only when asked, from the approved master's scenes.
