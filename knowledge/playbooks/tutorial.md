# Playbook: tutorial

A film that teaches someone to do a task themselves. The viewer should be able to follow along with the product open.

| | |
|---|---|
| Length | 2–4 min, in chapters of 20–45 s. Tuned on the first real tutorial |
| Energy preset | calm |
| Stage | Paper. Minimal effects |
| References to study first | `library/references/claude/`, the walkthrough clips in `cursor/` |

## Shape

| Part | Length | Job |
|---|---|---|
| Promise | 5–8 s | What you will have made by the end, shown finished |
| What you need | 3–5 s | Only if something is needed first |
| Chapters | 20–45 s each | One sub-task each, with a title card of two to four words |
| Recap | 5–8 s | The finished thing again, and the three steps named |
| Next | 3–5 s | Where to go from here, logo |

## Each step: say, show, confirm

1. **Say** what we are about to do and why (voice starts before the action).
2. **Show** it: where to click (medium), the click and what opens (macro), the result (medium).
3. **Confirm** with a short hold on the result so the viewer can check their own screen.

## Rules

- Accuracy over polish. Every click must work in the real product, in the order shown.
- Record the walkthrough at real speed with the scripted cursor. Speed changes happen in the edit and are labelled.
- No step longer than 8 s on screen. Typing longer than 2 s is sped up.
- Names of buttons and menus appear exactly as the product spells them, and the voice says them the same way.
- Callouts are drawn marks (ring, underline, arrow), one at a time, in the accent colour.
- Chapters must work alone: each one can be cut out and posted by itself.
- Music is low and steady, or absent. Interface sounds are present and quiet.

## First animation

One whole chapter, finished.

## Product knowledge

A tutorial is the deepest look the studio takes at a feature. Everything learned goes into `brands/<brand>/product/` with the date, and the tutorial's step list is stored so it can be re-checked when the product changes.

## When the product changes

A tutorial has a `checked on` date. When the product knowledge for its feature changes, the tutorial is flagged for a re-walk.
