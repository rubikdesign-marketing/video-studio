# Playbook: feature demo

A film that shows one feature doing one real job from start to finish. The viewer should think "I could do that".

| | |
|---|---|
| Length | 60–90 s. Cap agreed at the idea stage; tuned on the first real demo |
| Energy preset | confident or calm |
| Stage | Paper or glass. The product is the star |
| References to study first | `library/references/claude/` (all three), then `google/` |

## Shape

| Part | Share | Job | Energy |
|---|---|---|---|
| Result first | first 3–5 s | Show the finished outcome, then say "here is how" | 4 |
| The job | 5–10% | Who needs this and what they are trying to get done, in one line | 2 |
| Three steps | 60–70% | Each step: ask, result, hold. One continuous story with one set of data | 3, 3, 4 |
| Payoff | 8–10% | Return to the finished outcome, now understood | 5 |
| Close | 4–6 s | What to do next, logo | 3 |

## Rules

- One feature, one job, one customer story, one set of data from first frame to last.
- Prefer continuous camera movement over cuts. The viewer should feel they never left the product.
- Every scene change is a hand-off object, a container transform or a cursor lead.
- The cursor is the guide. Where it goes, the camera follows.
- Idealise the product's motion; never invent its screens.
- Real waiting is shortened and labelled "Sped up".
- The voice explains what is happening and why it matters, never which button is being pressed.

## Before the storyboard

The product scout does the whole job in the demo workspace, with new boards and pages built for this video, and records every screen and state. The storyboard is drawn from those captures, not from memory.

## First animation

The first 15 seconds at full polish (result-first opening and the first step), plus the animatic of the rest.

## What to record in the ledger

The feature, the customer story, the data set, the opening device and the payoff shot.
