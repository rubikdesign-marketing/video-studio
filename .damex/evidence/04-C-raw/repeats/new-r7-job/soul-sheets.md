# Saved replies: three ideas

Brief, in their words: "A 30 second 16:9 promo for the demo brand announcing a new feature: saved replies for the support inbox. Five scenes. Voice-over in the brand voice."

Settled by their words: 16:9, 30 s cap, voice-over (Justin, the demo brand's only voice), five scenes, launch promo.
Said back for correction: the film is for support teams, and after one watch they should know the support inbox now has saved replies and want to try them.

**Two limits the idea has to respect.** The demo brand has no product folder and no discovery sheet, so there is no real inbox to film (studio rule 10: real screens only). Every idea below is therefore drawn motion and type, with no mock-up of a screen, and says nothing about how the feature works beyond what you told me: you save a reply and use it again. Stage 0B (product discovery) was skipped on purpose for the same reason: there is nothing to discover for a test brand. If this ever becomes a real film, it needs real captures first.

## Idea A (recommended): "Typed again"

| | |
|---|---|
| Type | promo (launch) |
| Where it runs | Website and email launch post |
| Size and length cap | 16:9, 30 s |
| Energy preset | confident |
| Voice | Justin |
| Design style | Quiet editorial type on the off-white page, one deep green accent. Reason: the pain is dull and repeated, so the film is calm and clear, not loud |
| Hook family | Callout (names the viewer's own habit) |
| Flow pattern | pain, reframe, promise (`pain-reframe-promise`) |

**One sentence.** "Our support inbox finally stops making us type the same answer."

**Feeling.** Relief.

**One idea.** The same reply is typed out again and again, in a stack that keeps growing. Then it is written once, set aside as a single clean card, and the stack never starts again.

**Signature moment.** The growing stack of identical reply lines collapses into one card with a small green mark, in one smooth move, as the voice says "write it once".

**Stage.** Off-white page, dark ink type, one green accent, amber used once for the repeated lines. Nothing that looks like a screen.

**Never.** 1. A fake inbox or any invented screen. 2. A claim about speed, time saved or a number we cannot source. 3. "Introducing", "effortless", dashes between clauses.

**Different from the last five.** The ledger is empty, so there is nothing to repeat. Hook, look and move are all first uses for this brand.

**New to the library.** "Stack collapses to one card" (a repetition-to-single-object move).

**Facts that must be right.** Feature name: "saved replies". Where: the support inbox. Nothing else is claimed. Source: the brief.

## Idea B: "Before and after"

Hook family: demonstration (`before-after-on-screen`). Pattern: world, hero, transformation. Style: split page, left side busy, right side calm.
One idea: the same support question appears on a cluttered left half, answered by hand, then on a calm right half, answered from a saved reply. Feeling: order.
Why it is second: a split screen asks the viewer to compare two inboxes, and with no real inbox to film it would end up looking like a mock-up screen.

## Idea C: "Answer once"

Hook family: contrarian (`advice-rejected`). Pattern: wide claim, zoom, snap back. Style: big kinetic type, dark green night page.
One idea: "Everyone says answer faster. Answer once instead." Type carries the whole film. Feeling: confidence.
Why it is third: a contrarian hook needs proof inside fifteen seconds, and we have no result to point to.

## Recommendation

Idea A. It needs no data we do not have, the pain is one the viewer recognises in the first two seconds, and its signature move can be drawn honestly.
