## One sentence
"They've added saved replies, so I only write each answer once."

## Feeling
Relief, then a little pride. The support person gets their time back.

## One idea
**Type it once.** The same answer is typed again and again, then collapses into one short name that brings the whole answer back with a single entry.

## Signature moment
Scene 4, about three seconds. A short name is typed and the full answer unrolls in the reply box in one smooth move. The typing sound stops and a single soft click lands as the answer settles.

## Stage
A calm light page (the brand's off-white) with the deep green accent. Support work is quiet, repeated and a bit tiring, so the film is airy and ordered, not loud. The accent green carries the saved reply. The amber partner colour is used once, on the repeated typing in the hook.

## Design style
Quiet and typographic, with the product reply box as the hero. One line of reasoning: there is no product to show off, and the whole idea is words, so type does the work. (To be checked against the six styles in `13-design-styles.md` at the moodboard.)

## Flow pattern
Problem, then one clean ask, result and hold, then the payoff. One flow: save a reply, then use it. Not yet walked in a real product.

## Hook family
Problem. Two other openings are kept as ad variants: outcome first, and question.

## Never
1. Never invent a screen. If the real saved-replies UI is not captured, the scene is held until it is.
2. Never claim a speed, time saved or number nobody measured.
3. Never show a person (rule 23) or a third-party logo.
