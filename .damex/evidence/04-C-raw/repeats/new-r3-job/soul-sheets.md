# Soul sheets: Saved replies (demo brand, 30 s promo, 16:9)

Brief: "A 30 second 16:9 promo for the demo brand announcing a new feature: saved replies for the support inbox. Five scenes. Voice-over in the brand voice."

Shared by all three: 16:9, 30 s cap (binding), voice-over by Justin (the only voice on the demo brand), confident energy, hook inside 3 s with the words on screen. Five scenes in the promo shape: hook, turn, proof, peak, close.

Brand voice: the demo brand has no written voice (`brand.md` and `rules.md` are silent on it). Used here: plain, warm, spoken to one support person. Said back so you can correct it.

Honest limit: the demo brand has no product (`brands/demo/product/` says so), so no real saved-replies screen exists to capture. See "Facts" and the storyboard note at the end.

**One sentence.** "They turned typing the same answer into one keystroke."

**Feeling.** Relief.

**One idea.** The reply a support person has typed a hundred times, shown the way it feels: the same sentence, over and over, until one keystroke turns the pile into a single reply.

**Signature moment.** Scene 3. The same sentence is stacked down the screen, line after line, each a little dimmer. A slash appears and the whole stack folds into one bright green chip labelled "Refund policy". A quiet tick, no whoosh.

**Stage.** Dark green night page, bright green accent. Low-lit, like a late shift.

**Never.**
1. A made-up inbox screen passed off as the real product.
2. People or hands (rule 23).
3. "Seamless", "supercharge", or an opener like "Introducing" or "Meet".

## Concept B: "Typed versus saved"

Hook family: comparison, Head to head (`head-to-head`). Flow: split-screen race over the same ten tickets.
Idea: two columns of the same tickets, one answered by hand, one with saved replies. The right column finishes while the left is still typing.
Signature: the right column hits done and the left stops mid-word.
Energy: hype. Design style: split stage, light page against dark page.
Why not first: a race needs real timings and no product exists to time. We would be building on a number we cannot measure. Hook-library trap: a comparison with no honest ending.

## Concept C: "Not your typing speed"

Hook family: callout, Audience then reason (`audience-reason-not`). Flow: reframe (pain, reframe, promise).
Idea: replies feel slow because finding the words is slow, not typing. A shelf of answers replaces the hunting.
Signature: a search for "that old ticket" gets lost in a pile, and a single slash replaces it.
Energy: calm. Design style: warm light page, quiet.
Why not first: it argues against a belief we have no evidence the viewers hold, and the claim needs a source we do not have.

## Recommendation: A

It proves the claim with something the viewer sees instead of a number we cannot source, needs no invented screen, opens on the feeling inside three seconds, and is the easiest to do well in type alone.
