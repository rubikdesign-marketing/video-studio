# Study card: Claude — Your apps have moved into Claude

File: `library/references/claude/claude_launch_your-apps-have-moved-into-claude_60s.mp4` · 60.2 s · 3840x2160 · 24 fps
Studied: 2026-11-11 · Depth: frame by frame (5 overview sheets at 2 fps, 33 frame-by-frame windows, 3 low-rate sheets, all in `library/references/claude/_study/apps-moved/`)

Sizes below are a share of frame width (W) or height (H), read off 400 px thumbnails, so treat them as ±1 point. "f" means frames at 24 fps.

## 1. What it is, in one paragraph
A 60 second launch film telling existing Claude users that third-party work apps (a document tool, an image editor, a file store and about ten more) now open and work inside the chat. The one idea is in the first line of type, "Your apps have moved in": an app icon is a door, and behind every door is the same chat window doing real work in that app. It shows the idea three times with three different jobs (write into a doc, edit a photo, fix a floor plan), then sprays the whole icon set past to say "and all of these". The feeling is light and quick: a paper-white page, one drawn cursor that behaves like a small character, and almost no words.

## 5. Techniques worth taking

### The icon is a door
- **Seen at:** 11.5–12.08, 24.25–25.0 (in); 22.38–23.25, 47.75–49.25 (out).
- **What happens:** An icon tile (17.5% W) sits in front of a miniature of the whole app window. On the way in, both scale up together; the tile slides right and leaves frame while the window grows from tile size to full frame. The window holds live UI from the first frame, so there is no swap. On the way out it runs backwards: the full-frame UI gains rounded corners, shrinks to a card, keeps shrinking to tile size, and the tile slides back over it while neighbouring icons arrive from both sides.
- **Easing:** In: tile width 80, 86, 92, 100, 110, 120, 150, 175 px per frame (accelerating), then 6 f of settling. Out: 9 f slow start, 5 f fast middle, 7–12 f long settle.
- **Why it works:** The viewer never loses where they are. "This app is inside that icon" is shown as a physical fact, and getting back to the menu costs under a second.
- **Use it for:** Any film with several features or integrations behind one menu, hub or dashboard.
- **Rebuild:** Remotion: one wrapper with `transform: scale()`; put the real UI component and the tile inside as siblings, tile on top. Drive scale from 0.175 to 1 with `interpolate` and `Easing.bezier(0.7, 0, 0.3, 1)` over 14 f, and the tile's `translateX` from 0 to about 120% of card width over the same frames. Render the UI at full size and scale it down, so it is sharp at the end. HyperFrames: same DOM, one timeline tween on the wrapper and one on the tile. Card radius about 4% W, soft shadow.

### Momentum cut
- **Seen at:** 17.04, 26.63, 28.92, 41.04, 43.88.
- **What happens:** The outgoing shot starts to slide in one direction for 3–14 f, getting faster. Hard cut. The incoming shot is already moving the same direction and slows to a stop over 6–8 f. Travel is small: 2–12% of the frame before the cut, 2–8% after.
- **Easing:** Pure ease-in before the cut, pure ease-out after. Nothing is ever at rest on the cut frame.
- **Why it works:** It joins two zoom levels of a UI without a visible zoom, which would blur small text. The shared motion makes a cut feel like one continuous gesture.
- **Use it for:** Jumping between parts of a screen recording or UI rebuild: input to result, chat to side panel.
- **Rebuild:** Remotion: two back-to-back `<Sequence>`s. Shot A: `translate` by `interpolate(f, [end-7, end], [0, -0.08*W], {easing: Easing.in(Easing.cubic)})`. Shot B: from `+0.05*W` to 0 with `Easing.out(Easing.cubic)` over 8 f. Keep the sign of travel the same. HyperFrames: two clips, two tweens, no crossfade.

### Two zoom levels, cut in on the cursor
- **Seen at:** 12.08–13.5 then 13.5; 34.5–36.08 then 36.08; 43.88 onward.
- **What happens:** The whole app window is shown full-frame for only 0.75–1.5 s (text about 1% H, unreadable, there to show the place). Then a straight cut to a close-up where one element fills the frame: reply box about 83% W, typed text about 3% H, or a doc title about 5.5% H. Wide shots return only when the content is a picture (photo, floor plan).
- **Easing:** None; cuts. At 13.5 the cursor is travelling down-left in both shots.
- **Why it works:** Real UI is too dense for video. Showing the place and then one readable thing respects both.
- **Use it for:** Every product demo beat.
- **Rebuild:** Build the UI once at a large logical size; define named camera rects (wide, input, doc) and cut between them. Do not tween between rects unless it is the door move.

### Stepped fisheye icon row
- **Seen at:** 2.6–6.3.
- **What happens:** Seven icons in a row under the logo tile. The centre icon is about 11% W, its neighbours 8% W, the outer ones smaller and faded to about 30%. The row moves one place (about 11.5% W) in 5–6 f, then holds about 18 f with a very slow creep, then steps again. As it first appears it is already sliding fast (about 5% W per frame) and slows into the stepping.
- **Easing:** Each step ease-in-out; the entrance is a long ease-out.
- **Why it works:** Each app gets about a second in the bright centre slot, so twelve logos are readable without a logo wall.
- **Use it for:** Integrations, templates, customer logos, feature lists.
- **Rebuild:** `pos = floor(f/24) + ease(clamp((f%24 - 18)/6))`; x = (i − pos) × pitch. Scale = `interpolate(|d|, [0,1,3], [1.25,1,0.85])`, opacity = `interpolate(|d|, [2,3.5], [1,0])` where d is distance from centre in places.

### Fling, swirl and burst round a fixed anchor
- **Seen at:** 6.29–8.04.
- **What happens:** The cursor changes to a grabbing pose, pulls the row left for 4 f, then flings. The row breaks into a ring of icons circling a small "Add" button that appears at centre. The ring turns about 30° per quarter second, widens from about 20% W radius to 35% W while the icons grow, then accelerates out of frame in 6 f. The headline words fall and fade one at a time. Only the button is left.
- **Easing:** Ring radius ease-in (slow widening, sudden exit). Animated on twos.
- **Why it works:** Chaos with a still centre. The eye is delivered to the one thing to click.
- **Use it for:** Turning a list into a single call to action; clearing a busy frame.
- **Rebuild:** Polar coordinates per icon: angle = a0 + ω·t, radius = r0 + k·t³; scale rises with radius. Keep the anchor element outside the rotating group.

### Click with weight, then a small reward
- **Seen at:** 1.92–2.25 (tile), 8.88–9.5 (button), 20.88 (menu).
- **What happens:** The pressed thing shrinks (tile to about 88% for 7 f, button for 2 f) and springs back in 1 f. On "Add", about 12 outlined shapes (capsules, dashes, squares in rose, green and amber) appear about 10% W from the button as long streaks, shorten to dots within 3 f, and drift outward to about 16% W while fading over 18 f.
- **Easing:** Burst distance 40, 45, 55, 60, 65 (thumbnail px): very fast out, exponential slowdown.
- **Why it works:** The click is felt, not just seen. The burst is small and gone in under a second.
- **Use it for:** The one click in a film that matters (add, install, publish).
- **Rebuild:** Press: scale 1 → 0.9 → 1 with a 2–7 f hold. Particles: r = R·(1 − e^(−t/4)); stretch 3 → 1 over 3 f; opacity 1 → 0 from frame 8 to 18; stroke only, no fill.

### A drawn cursor as the lead character, on twos
- **Seen at:** 0.21–56.0 almost without a break (absent 24.7–26.6 and 41.0–43.9).
- **What happens:** The cursor is an outlined arrow, large: 10–13% H in title and close-up scenes, 5–6% H in wide UI. It turns to face where it is going, takes a grab pose, and on fast moves becomes a solid black brush smear for 2–6 f. In the playful scenes it, the swirl, the confetti and the final icon race hold each drawing for 2 frames (12 per second); product UI moves every frame.
- **Easing:** Arcs, never straight lines; overshoots and loops before landing; lands with 4–6 f of slowing.
- **Why it works:** It gives a UI film a protagonist and carries the eye through every transition. The two frame rates separate "play" from "product" without any colour or sound cue.
- **Use it for:** Any screen-led film that needs warmth.
- **Rebuild:** Path as an SVG curve; position and tangent from `@remotion/paths` (`getPointAtLength`, `getTangentAtLength`); rotate the sprite to the tangent. Quantise with `f2 = Math.floor(frame/2)*2`. Swap to a stretched blob sprite when travel per 2 f exceeds about 4% W. Draw a Tulvo cursor; do not reuse this one.

### Pull back, slide, push in
- **Seen at:** 32.0–34.5.
- **What happens:** Full-frame UI shrinks to a rounded card at about 78% W (18 f) and almost pauses. The card slides left as the next window's card enters from the right, gap about 12% W (26 f). Before the slide has finished the new card starts growing to full frame (14 f). The old cursor leaves with the old card.
- **Easing:** Slide: 6 f ease-in then a 20 f exponential ease-out (each frame covers about 85% of the previous frame's distance). The three moves overlap by 3–4 f.
- **Why it works:** It says "a different conversation" where the door move says "a different app". Two transitions, two meanings.
- **Use it for:** Moving between projects, accounts, sites or users.
- **Rebuild:** A world container with both cards side by side; animate camera scale 1 → 0.78, x by one card plus gap, scale back to 1, with offsets so they overlap. Faint background tint shift is optional.

### Writing that streams, with a named caret
- **Seen at:** 17.5–19.6 (doc), 26.7–28.6 and 45.8–47.3 (chat).
- **What happens:** In the doc, a pale panel fades in (3 f), pushes the rows below down at once, and text appears one word per frame with a small coral name tag riding the insertion point; each new line pushes the layout down again. In chat, several lines fill left to right and the newest 6–8 words are coral, turning black behind the front.
- **Easing:** Linear, 24 words a second.
- **Why it works:** The tag says who is writing without a caption. The coloured front shows where to look in a block of small text.
- **Use it for:** Any "the tool writes it for you" moment.
- **Rebuild:** Slice words by frame; colour each word by age (`interpolate(age, [0,6], [accent, ink])`). Place the tag from measured text (`@remotion/layout-utils` `measureText`, or a ref). Let real layout reflow; do not fake the push.

### Typing to a time budget, then a reading hold
- **Seen at:** 14.1–16.0 (36 characters, about 19 a second); 36.3–38.95 (about 230 characters, about 87 a second) then a 1.5 s hold.
- **What happens:** Every typed request takes 2–2.7 s whatever its length. The long one is followed by 1.5 s with nothing moving but the cursor. The second demo skips typing: the request arrives as a finished chip.
- **Easing:** Linear.
- **Why it works:** Pace stays even; the viewer gets time to read after the typing, not during it.
- **Use it for:** Any prompt or form entry.
- **Rebuild:** `chars = floor(interpolate(f, [0, dur], [0, text.length]))` with dur 48–64 f; add hold = about 0.4 s per line of text.

### Honest waiting: one line and a running timer
- **Seen at:** 41.04–43.88.
- **What happens:** The screen is white with a single grey status line (text about 3% H, about 78% W) and a small coral animated mark. The line's text changes five times in 2.8 s; a lighter band sweeps across it; a timer at its end runs from 1m 35s to 2m 9s. Then a cut to the result, which changes state in one frame (yellow marks appear).
- **Easing:** Text swaps are instant.
- **Why it works:** It admits the job takes minutes while spending three seconds on it, and tells the viewer what the tool is doing meanwhile.
- **Use it for:** Any long-running job: builds, imports, AI generation.
- **Rebuild:** Array of status strings, switch every 14–18 f; timer = start + f × 12 s/s; shimmer as a moving gradient mask.

### Rotating word in a highlight box
- **Seen at:** 52.0–55.0.
- **What happens:** The tagline fades in word by word (2–3 f stagger). Its first word sits in a pale blue box. The word changes three times: the old word slides up and out, the new one rises in, inside the box, in 3 f; the box changes width and grows to the left so the rest of the sentence never moves. Each word holds 11–13 f. Then the box fades (3 f).
- **Easing:** Fast, near linear.
- **Why it works:** Four promises in the space of one line, and the eye never has to re-find the sentence.
- **Use it for:** Taglines with a list in them.
- **Rebuild:** Fixed sentence; an `overflow: hidden` inline box right-aligned against it; `translateY` ±100% over 3 f; box width interpolated between measured word widths.

### The row hands over to the type; the mark grows from a dot
- **Seen at:** 49.75–52.4 and 56.08–57.63.
- **What happens:** The icon race (ease-in 0.75 s, top speed about 1.6 frame widths a second, ease-out 0.9 s, on twos) ends with the last icon leaving left and the tagline fading in where the next icon would be, still drifting 2% W left. Later the words fade out left to right (14 f), one blank frame, a dot (0.5% W) holds 3 f, opens into the mark with a slight turn (15 f), shifts left, and the wordmark types on one letter per frame (5 f).
- **Easing:** As above.
- **Why it works:** Every element arrives from the motion before it; nothing just appears.
- **Use it for:** Endings.
- **Rebuild:** Treat the tagline as the last item in the row's coordinate system. End card: scale from 0.07 to 1 with rotation −20° to 0, then a per-letter reveal.

## 6. Camera
There are only three camera moves: through an icon (in or out), pull back / slide / push in between two windows, and the short drifts either side of a momentum cut. Each is under 1.5 s. Between them the frame is locked; inside a demo all change of view is by straight cut. A move is always triggered by something finishing (a click, a result landing), never by time alone. What never happens: slow continuous zoom on UI, pans across a screen, tilt or 3D rotation, crossfades, blur.

## 7. Product UI treatment
- Real product UI, full bleed, no device or browser frame. When seen from outside it is a white card with large rounded corners and a soft shadow on the page colour.
- Two crops only: whole window, or one element. In close-ups the element sits in the lower middle of frame with a band of page colour below it.
- At most one thing changes at a time: text streams, or a menu opens, or a slider moves.
- Cursor: large, drawn, rotates; lives in UI space on the way in (shrinks with the card at 22.4) but stays full size on the last exit (48.4).
- Click feedback: the UI's own states (focus ring on the toolbar button, send button turning coral when text is present). No added ripples inside UI.
- One screen becomes the next by cut, or by layout push (new block shoves rows down in one frame).
- Results that are pictures get the frame: photo 26% W × 74% H; floor plan 50% W. The slider that changes the photo is tiny; the photo is the feedback (four visibly different states in 1.2 s).

## 8. Type and captions
- Two voices: a warm serif for the film's own words, the product's plain sans inside UI.
- Only three lines of film type in 60 s: headline (5 words), tagline (6 words), wordmark. About 5% H tall, 44–50% W wide, centred, black on the page colour.
- In: word by word, grey to black. Out: word by word, or words dropping away. No captions, no labels, no arrows on the UI.
- No voice captions are shown; whether there is a voice is unknown (not heard).

## 9. Colour, background, light, texture
- Page: warm off-white, sampled `#F9F6F3` / `#F7F7F3`. App surfaces: `#FDFDFD`. Ink: `#121212`.
- Accent coral (logo tile, send button, name tag, streaming front, end mark): sampled `#D6714F`–`#D87658`.
- Tagline highlight: `#ACC9D0`. Doc panel: about `#E8F0FC`. Change marks on the plan: `#F8E868`. Confetti: green `#609685`, plus rose and amber (approximate, read from sheets).
- All other colour comes from the app icons and the content (blue sky photo, green and grey floor plan).
- Flat: no gradients, no grain, no glow. Depth only from soft shadows under tiles and cards and from things passing in front of each other.

## 11. Principles
1. One sentence of idea, shown as a physical move (the icon opens), then repeated three times with different content.
2. Never show a whole app window for more than 1.5 s unless the content is a picture; cut to one readable element.
3. Join UI views with cuts that carry motion across them; do not zoom or pan across small text.
4. Give every transition a meaning and use each for one meaning only (through the icon = another app; slide sideways = another conversation).
5. Keep one object on screen from start to finish to lead the eye (here the cursor) and let it enter each new scene first.
6. Rest the picture where the viewer must read: at least 1.5 s of stillness after a long prompt.
7. Type every request in 2–3 s regardless of length; show waiting as one line with a running clock.
8. Keep film type to three lines of six words or fewer; the product does the rest of the talking.
9. Keep the page, ink and one accent constant; let all other colour come from the content.
10. Use a lower frame rate (12 a second) for playful moments and full rate for product, so the two never feel like the same material.

## 12. Not to copy
- The coral spark mark, the coral logo tile, the "Claude" wordmark and its serif typeface.
- The Claude chat interface itself: layout, reply box, status line wording, model picker, name tag.
- The third-party app icons and their order.
- The exact drawn cursor (shape, brush-smear frames, poses). Take the idea of a cursor with character; draw a different one.
- The warm paper page with coral accent as a pairing, and the confetti palette.
- The lines "Your apps have moved in" and "… at the speed of thought".
- The sample content (project names, the photo, the floor plan).
